# 🧪 T-21: `&bk` vs raw `&kp` after a multi-step replace macro

> **Status: complete.** 2 spec builds + 6 supplementary builds, all built and ran. The logs are deterministic: re-running `a` and `g` gave the same md5.
> **Verdict: `deviates`.**
> - Both spec builds produced **byte-identical** logs, and neither shows the predicted order. The follower key is never resolved on layer 1. It lands on **layer 0** 1 ms after layer 1 turns off, so the `&bk L` / `&kp L` binding under test is never reached.
> - The supplementary builds partly confirm the mechanism. Raw `&kp` does jump ahead of queued output. `&bk` keeps order only behind items **already in the queue**. The nested `&ak` inside the replace macro adds its B only when its own queue item runs, so a `&bk` follower delivered earlier than that lands **before B**.

---

## 🎯 Result first

- ❌ **Spec builds (a) `&bk L` and (b) `&kp L` on layer 1 pos 3 give the same output.** md5 `ff0172960cb1e6d8064d1f22fbfd36e1` for both, and the snapshots are identical too.
  - D-key delivered at **t+59** as `layer_id: 0 position: 3, binding name: key_press` (base `&kp D`).
  - Layer 1 went **off at t+58**, at the ⌫ key-up. The ⌫ key-down was the first keycode after A's sticky layer was armed, so it consumed that layer.
  - HID in both: **A, ⌫, L, D, B**. The spec predicted `A, ⌫, L, B, L` (bk) and `A, ⌫, L, L, B` (raw).
- ⚠️ **The E-key does not start the replace at t+31.** It is held by the combo engine (S16, Q18 and the hack are all candidates).
  - Spec timing: the replace starts at **t+47**, when the D-key's press flushes the E-key.
  - Late timing: the replace starts at **t+50**, from the E-key's own Q18 timer.
- ✅ **Raw `&kp` jumps ahead of queued macro output.**
  - (d): raw D↓ at t+77 comes before B↓ at t+83.
  - (h): raw M↓ at t+43 comes before the macro's own L↓ at t+54, while ⌫ is still held.
- ⚠️ **`&bk` keeps FIFO order only per queue item, and a nested macro's items are enqueued late.**
  - (e) follower delivered at t+77, after `ak(3,B)↓` ran at t+72 → D↓ at **t+94**, after B → **A ⌫ L B D**. This is the order the spec intends.
  - (c) follower delivered at t+59, before `ak↓` ran at t+69 → D↓ at **t+80**, before B↓ at t+91 → **A ⌫ L D B**. The same letter order as raw.
  - (f) in-window follower (layer 1 `&bk L`) delivered at t+43 → follower L↓ at **t+76**, before B↓ at t+87 → **A ⌫ L L B**.
- ℹ️ **A raw `&kp` of the same letter the macro types is not dropped (g).**
  - `hid_listener` pre-releases the key: `unregistering … keycode 0x0F since it was already pressed` at t+54.
  - The host therefore sees two L key-downs.
  - The macro's L↑ at t+65 releases the follower's L even though that key is physically held until t+360.
- 🆕 **Dead zone after a replace macro (observed in a, c, d, e).**
  - Any key delivered between ⌫↑ (F+11 native / F+10 board) and the nested `aksl` (F+44 native / F+40 board) resolves on **layer 0**.
  - Neither AK layer is on at that point: the old one was consumed by ⌫, and the new one (layer 3 here) is not on yet.

## 📟 Board translation (MODEL §(a))

- Rule: every timer-driven step loses its native +1. Combo rungs fire at deadline + δ, δ ∈ (0.03, 1.05] ms. Queue ticks fire at 10 ms + 1 tick (≈ 10.03). Key-driven steps (the D-key press flushing the E-key in a/b/c) have no offset.
- **The ordering is identical on the nRF52840 in all 8 cases.** The margins that decide each case:
  - **a/b/c, D-key delivery vs layer 1 OFF.** Both are scheduled from the same handler at F: the combo uses `K_MSEC(11)` → ≈ F+11.05, the queue uses `K_MSEC(10)` → ≈ F+10.04. So the D-key still arrives about 1 ms after layer 1 drops and resolves on base. It is a 1 ms margin on both targets.
  - **d/e.** The D-key is delivered at press+11+δ = t+76 (board). That is between `ak↓` at F+20 = t+69 and B↓ at F+30 = t+79. The order is unchanged: raw jumps ahead of B, `&bk` lands after it.
  - **f/g/h.** The board bypass needs Δ ≤ 9. Δ = 6 holds, so the follower resolves on layer 1 inside the E-key's dispatch, as on native.
- **Board times (t = A-key press):**
  - a/b: A↓ t+16, layer 1 ON t+26, ⌫↓ t+47, ⌫↑ + L↓ t+57, D↓ t+58, `ak↓` t+67, B↓ t+77, layer 3 ON t+87.
  - c: the buffered D↓ at t+77, B↓ t+87.
  - f: follower L↓ t+72, B↓ t+82.
- **Not modelled by native:** debounce (≈5 ms per edge) and the BLE split latency for left-hand keys. They shift the absolute times, not the queue order.
