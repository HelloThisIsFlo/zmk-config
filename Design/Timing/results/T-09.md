## 🔌 Board translation (native +1 ms removed; MODEL §(a))

| case | native HID ↓ | board HID ↓ (engine time, t = debounced timestamp of key 1) | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| a / a2 (hack on, Δ = 6) | t+17 | **t+16 + δ**, δ ∈ (0.03, 1.05] ms | timer-driven. The hack rung runs at t+11+δ, re-arms `K_MSEC(t+16 − k_uptime_get())`, and R16's rung lands at t+16 + δ. |
| b (hack on, Δ = 13) | t+13 | **t+13** (exact, same as the key's timestamp) | key-driven. The hack rung at t+11+δ precedes A at t+13 on both targets, so the outcome is the same. |
| c (hack off, Δ = 6) | t+6 | **t+6** (exact) | key-driven; no timer involved. |

- `&bk C` key-up: native +11 after ↓ → board +10 + δ.
- Both keys are on the same half, so debounce (4–6 ms) shifts both presses alike and keeps Δ within ±1 ms. The t on the board is about 5 ms after the physical contact.
- **Board-only nuances outside this test's inputs:**
  - At Δ = 11, A's press and the hack rung (t+11+δ) fall in the same ms. The result is the same either way (the hack is filtered out by `timeout_at ≤ ts` on the key path or dropped by the rung), so the combo fires at t+11.
  - At Δ = 12 on native, A's press and the hack rung share `[t+12]`, with the same outcome.
  - Left-half keys reach the central over BLE and can shift the apparent Δ across the 11 ms line (MODEL §(d) cross-hand table). R16-type combos on Flo's board are same-hand.
- **Hack latency on a real combo, stated for the page:** a 2-key 16 ms combo typed with both keys inside 11 ms is emitted at **t+16** with the hack instead of at completion (t+Δ). That is a 10 ms + δ delay at Δ = 6, and in general 16 − Δ. For an 18 ms combo (RM1–RM4 delWord/capsWord siblings) the analogous deadline is t+18. 18 ms siblings were not exercised here.
